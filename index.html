04 — why zine reading works

Pages, not an endless scroll

Natural stopping points

A page has an edge. Scroll has none — so a page is a place your mind can safely pause, without losing your place.

Spatial memory

"It was near the top of the left page" is a real memory cue in a paginated book — and a meaningless one in a scroll that never repeats a layout.

Less cognitive load

A fixed two-page spread is a bounded, predictable unit. Infinite scroll keeps re-rendering that boundary away, which costs attention you'd rather spend on the words.

07 — faq

Questions, answered

What is Zine?

A reading workspace for PDFs and text/markdown files, built around one idea: reading is better as a calm, focused, page-by-page experience than a wall of infinite scroll — and better still when your progress visibly adds up to something, your Mind Forest.

Why is it different from a normal PDF reader?

Most PDF readers are just viewers. Zine pairs the reading surface with a built-in Pomodoro timer, ambient sound, and a progression system that turns consistent reading into a visible, growing forest — the reading experience is the whole product, not an afterthought.

Why does Zine improve focus and comprehension?

Two-page, edge-bounded layouts give your mind natural stopping points and spatial memory cues that an endless scroll never repeats — see "why zine reading works" above. Layer in a distraction-free workspace, a focus timer, and non-verbal ambient sound, and there's simply less pulling your attention off the page.

How does the Mind Forest work?

Reading pages, keeping a daily streak, crossing book milestones, and finishing Pomodoro focus sessions all earn Growth Points. Growth Points unlock an ever-growing catalog of trees, plants, and wildlife — every unlock is tied to the specific book and session that earned it, so your forest is a real record, not a random decoration.

How does the Pomodoro system help?

A focus/break cycle built into the reader itself — start it before you read, and a completed focus session feeds straight into your Mind Forest. It's there to reward sustained attention, not speed-reading.

What makes the reading experience calming and immersive?

A quiet, distraction-free workspace; procedurally generated ambient sound (rain, forest, café, ocean, library, fireplace, wind, night) with no music or speech to compete for attention; and a page-turn interaction that feels like a real book rather than a scrollbar.

Why should I sign in?

Signing in with Google is what lets Zine keep your Mind Forest, reading history, and streaks — and carry them to any device you read from next. Your documents themselves are never uploaded; only your reading progress syncs.

Is my data private?

Your files stay in your browser — they're never uploaded anywhere. Signing in only syncs metadata about your reading activity (session lengths, pages read, growth events) to your account, protected behind your own Google sign-in.
